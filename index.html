The Short Version

The party's two big surges, 2016 and 2020, came from presidential-year attention, and the LNC kept neither. Dues-paying membership peaked at 20,215 in Jan 2021 and has fallen ever since, to 8,816 in Apr 2026, the lowest in the record. Individual donations in 2025, $0.81M, were the lowest in the FEC record back to 2006.

The record points to three habits rather than any single bad vote:

  1. The LNC sets goals and almost never scores them. A 2006 rule required an annual review "to set metrics and monitors". It was dropped by 2009. Only two terms in twenty years were ever measured against their goals.

  2. Each board resets what the last one built, and inherits contracts it did not choose. The D.C. 2024 convention is the clearest case. In 2024–26, 26 people passed through about 16 voting seats.

  3. Since 2018 the board has spent a growing share of its votes on fights with itself and its affiliates. Of the five that reached the convention or the Judicial Committee, three were reversed, one was upheld and one appeal was turned away without a hearing on the merits.

The decisions that held up share one trait. They were written down as standing rules and followed across several chairs. The mortgage paydown rule is the best example. The worst losses came where written rules already existed and were not followed.

Part 2 · Method

How The Links Are Graded

Consequences often land a year or more later, and usually on a different board. So each decision below is followed forward through the record, and every later step is graded by how strongly the record ties it back.

DocumentedPlausibleTiming Only

Documented means the minutes or list connect the two events themselves. Plausible means there is a clear mechanism and the timing fits. Timing only means an outcome moved afterwards and nothing else connects them. Only documented links are treated as causes.

The outcome charts above are the check on everything else. A decision "worked" if the number it was meant to move actually moved within one to three years, and nothing else that changed at the same time explains it better. Presidential campaigns are the usual competing explanation.

Part 4 · Attention

Where The Board's Attention Went

This shows what share of each term's decisions, LNC and Executive Committee together, were about outward work versus internal conflict. Outward work means candidates, ballot access, outreach, messaging and membership. Internal conflict means censures, suspensions, charges, trials, removals and affiliate disputes. Everything else (budgets, appointments, rules, contracts) is the remainder. The counts come from fixed keyword patterns over all 1,720 rows and are rough; the trend is clear even so.

Part 5 · Suggestions

What The Record Suggests

Each suggestion comes from a pattern above, not from a view about which faction is right.

Score The Goals At Every Meeting

Bring back the 2006 rule in a form that sticks: staff publish a one-page scorecard against the term's goals, with definitions fixed when the goals are adopted. The 2010–11 status reports show this can be done.

Make Goals Outlast The Board That Set Them

Adopt three-year targets that the next board must review before replacing. Every reset in the record, in 2009, 2021 and 2026, lost the work in progress.

Plan For The Year After The Surge

Require a retention plan within 90 days of each presidential election. Lapses peaked the year after both surges, and the 2017 drop was forecast to the board in April with nothing tried.

Enforce The Contract Rules Already Written

Written contracts (2011), related-party approval (2023) and the $3,000 committee rule (2025) are on the books. Have the Treasurer certify each quarter that every payment over the limit has its approval on file, and report the exceptions.

Hand Convention Contracts On With Their Terms

Give each incoming board a written brief on inherited site contracts, their penalty clauses and projected costs. Require an emergency-exit clause like Austin's in every new contract.

Price The Warning Before Changing Systems

Before any donor-system move, require the written analysis the board orders, including what happens to recurring card donors. The 2016 warning sat in the minutes for six years.

Raise The Cost Of Internal Discipline

Use the January 2025 trial procedure, and require an outside review before a suspension or disaffiliation takes effect. Most attempts that were reviewed were reversed, and each one cost members and officers.

Fix The $25 Problem

Separate the voting franchise from the fundraising product, or price it so it covers its cost. The board was shown in 2021 that it nets almost nothing, and membership revenue has since fallen by more than half.

Part 7 · Limits

Limits Of This Analysis

What This Can And Can't Show

The record shows what the LNC decided and what it was told. Many outcomes depend on things it did not control: the presidential candidate, the national political mood, COVID. Where those explain a change better, the link above is graded timing only.

Membership definitions changed in February 2023, May 2023 and January 2025, and some months in 2010–13 have no saved report. Membership revenue for 2019–2024 rests on one Treasurer's table read by OCR.

The vote record has holes: 90 minutes or email ballots with no readable votes, and 11 ballots with conflicting posted results. In July 2026 the LNC itself recorded that "The status of recent minutes is also unclear" (Minutes, 5 Jul 2026 p.25). Three 2026 meetings exist only as drafts. The November 2019 minutes are a scanned document, read with OCR.

From 2025 the Executive Committee made major decisions (litigation, counsel, a member's suspension). They are now counted in the attention table, but its minutes are thinner than the LNC's.

Where an original list post was never archived (Benedict's September 2016 email, the August 2016 ballot notice), the link goes to the earliest archived reply that quotes it in full.
